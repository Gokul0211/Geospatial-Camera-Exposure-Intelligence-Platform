# Raising the work to a Q1 journal: assessment, novelty audit, and plan

_Written 2026-10-09. Web results below come from search summaries, not from reading the full papers; every item must be
checked against the original before it is cited. "Verdict" means: what the searches did or did not find, not a legal or
definitive statement of novelty._

## 0. Bottom line

The current manuscript would very likely be rejected at IEEE TIFS or any comparable Q1 venue. The idea (do not trust a
camera on vulnerability data alone; also check whether its feed is authentic) is sound and, in my searches, **not covered as
a single fused camera trust score**. But the paper currently shows it with (a) a hand-written synthetic corpus, (b) hand-set
weights and thresholds, (c) a fusion rule (a product) that needs a justification beyond "we chose it", (d) no adaptive
adversary, and (e) detectors whose individual ideas have prior art that the paper does not cite.
A Q1 paper needs **real data, a principled core, and an adversary-aware evaluation**. Section 4 gives a package that gets there.
Even then, acceptance is not guaranteed; the package raises the chance, it does not secure it.

## 3. Candidate upgrades

Effort figures are my rough estimates for one person and are not commitments.

### U1. Make the fusion principled and calibrated, with a false-trip guarantee (core upgrade)
**Idea.** Define trust as the probability that a device is *both* secure *and* authentic: `T = P(secure) * P(authentic | evidence)`
under conditional independence. The product is then not a design choice but the AND-semantics of the requirement, additive
averaging corresponds to an OR/mixture, and the gate `G(s)` becomes a *calibration map* from raw integrity evidence to
`P(authentic)`. Each Channel-B detector outputs a nonconformity score; calibrate it on genuine-only data per device class
(Mondrian conformal) so that a trip has a guaranteed false-trip rate <= alpha under exchangeability, and combine tiers with
valid p-/e-value rules. Thresholds then come from alpha and a cost ratio, not from hand-tuning.
**Why it matters.** Answers weaknesses 3, 5, 10. It turns a heuristic score into a calibrated, auditable decision rule.
**Novelty check.** Conformal anomaly detection exists (Q4, Q11 results: Mondrian CAD, online COAD); no result combined it
with camera/device-fingerprint drift or with trust fusion. The "product = AND of independent conditions" framing is simple and
may be known in trust literature; **not searched specifically, check before claiming**.
**Risks.** Legitimate firmware updates break exchangeability (needs a benign-change model, e.g. per-vendor release calendars);
independence of the two channels is an assumption to test. **Effort:** 3-5 weeks.

### U2. Real replay/loop detection and an adaptive attacker (answers weakness 6, 7)
**Idea.** Add loop detection (periodicity / self-similarity of the hash sequence) to Tier 2. Build the attacks a smart adversary
would use: noise-injected frozen frame (defeats the Hamming <= 1 rule), a short loop of real footage, slow resolution/blur
drift, banner mimicry. Report which evidence defeats which attack. Offer PRNU and ENF as optional *evidence adapters* with
calibrated likelihood ratios rather than reinventing them.
**Novelty check.** Loop/replay detection is covered by ENF and SurFi (Q2, Q12), duplication-forensics literature, and PRNU work
(Q1). Individually **not novel**; the contribution is the adversary-aware evaluation inside a trust-fusion framework.
**Effort:** 3-4 weeks.

### U3. Real data, two kinds (answers weaknesses 1, 2, 4)
(a) **Longitudinal measurement.** Use Censys research access (about five years of daily snapshots, free for verified
researchers who publish) to measure, for camera hosts, how often banner fields change, how often several independent layers
change together, firmware transition patterns, and how often hosts look like honeypots. This yields the real benign-drift
distribution that U1 needs for calibration. **Novelty check:** no longitudinal camera firmware/substitution study found (Q15).
(b) **Controlled testbed.** A handful of owned IP cameras plus decoys/honeypots behind a lab public address, with swaps at known
times: real ground truth for substitution, loop injection and freeze, measured against real scanners and our own scanner.
Scanner cadence limits what public engines can see (reported fewer than 40 banner grabs per month in one comparison, Q3), so
detection latency must be reported against the scan interval.
**Effort:** (a) 3-4 weeks; (b) 4-6 weeks including setup. **Risk:** access approval; ISP/legal limits on exposing devices; keep all
third-party hosts anonymised (see `CLAUDE.md` ethics boundary).

### U4. Adaptive-adversary and cost analysis
**Idea.** Formalise attacker classes and report evasion cost, e.g. the number of independent fingerprint layers (banner, TLS
certificate / JARM-style handshake fingerprint, TCP/IP stack, ports) an attacker must reproduce to keep Drift under the gate,
and the query budget needed to learn the thresholds. Perceptual-hash evasion work shows hash-based detectors can be evaded under
some budgets and are robust under others (Q10), so budgets must be stated.
**Novelty check:** hash-evasion exists generally; not found for camera freeze/loop detectors or banner-drift gates. **Effort:** 2-3 weeks.

### U5. Decision-theoretic dispatch evaluation (optional)
**Idea.** Replace the fixed tier cut-offs by an expected-cost rule: dispatch iff `P(real event | trust-weighted evidence) * C_miss > (1-P) * C_false`.
Simulate attack campaigns on a multi-camera network and report avoided false dispatches and added misses.
**Novelty check:** cost-aware triage exists for SOCs (Q16 results) and trust-aware fusion exists (MATE); surveillance dispatch not found.
**Effort:** 2-3 weeks. **Value:** relevance to practice; not necessary for acceptance.

### Considered and not recommended as the core
- **ENF as the main Tier 2 signal.** Strong prior art, needs a grid reference and audio or a particular light source, and many cameras lack audio.
- **PRNU as the main signal.** Strong prior art, fragile under compression; good as an adapter.
- **More blockchain/ledger emphasis.** Lightweight blockchain for surveillance integrity already exists; the Merkle chain is plumbing here.
- **Deep or learned fusion.** No labelled field data yet; would overfit synthetic scenarios.
- **GNN collusion detector as a headline.** Trained on synthetic graphs only.

## 4. Recommended package

**U1 + U2 + U3 + U4** (U5 only if time allows). Reframed claim:

> Camera trust should be the calibrated probability that a device is both secure and authentic. We give a conformal, false-trip-controlled
> estimator of authenticity from passive scanner history and optional stream evidence, validate it on longitudinal real scans and a
> controlled testbed, and evaluate it against adaptive adversaries.

Expected structure: formal model and AND-semantics (U1) -> evidence adapters and calibration -> real-data benchmarks (U3) ->
adaptive attacks (U2, U4) -> ablations and operating points. The current IG-DCTF score becomes the transparent special case that
works without calibration data. My rough total: **12-18 weeks** of focused work.

## 5. Testing and data (details in `DATASETS.md`)
Yes, this work requires real testing; the present synthetic evaluation cannot carry a Q1 claim. Minimum: longitudinal scan data
(Censys), a controlled testbed, a public tamper dataset (UHCTD), generated freeze/loop/replay from clean video, and optionally ENF
and VISION for the PRNU/ENF adapters.
